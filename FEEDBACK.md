# Feedback → Product

How real discussions shaped veripaper. Every principle below came from someone
pushing back.

## 2026-10-16 · r/PhD launch thread

- **"Do you have a source for this?"** (smurferdigg, on the "12 of 30" hook)
  → Iron rule 4: the honesty system applies to the tool's own claims (dogfood).
  Illustrative numbers are labeled illustrative, never presented as measured.
- **"Use AI to fix AI hallucinations? Genius."** (Lazy_Dragonfly_5447)
  → Iron rule 5: lookup, not generation. The verifier generates nothing — it
  only queries databases and reports back.
- **"Destroys our planet and our intellect"** (Confidenceisbetter)
  → Iron rule 6: lightweight by design. The heaviest operation is an HTTP GET.
- **"A reference can be real and still not support the claim"** (Far-Campaign-8902)
  → Iron rule 7: nuance over binary. Report *which parts* are supported, with
  quotes — never a flat yes/no.
- **"Why bother with a PhD if AI does everything?"** (TopPercentage)
  → Thesis line: the tool automates bureaucratic taxes on thinking, not thinking
  itself. It can't give you taste; taste is the whole PhD.
