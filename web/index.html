Abstract

veripaper is an open-source workbench that verifies the citations in a research draft. It looks up whether each cited source exists (CrossRef, DataCite, arXiv, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC, DBLP), resolves reference-list markers to identifiers, and — with your own API key — judges whether a source's abstract substantively supports the claim. Every claim receives an honest label: verified, cited, unverified, or stale.

It never generates content and never pretends. When evidence is uncertain, it downgrades the label and leaves the judgment to the author.

§1

Ground a draft

Paste a markdown draft, or upload a .docx / .pdf — text is extracted in your browser, never uploaded. Or paste / upload a BibTeX .bib file to check every entry (DOI / arXiv / title lookup). Each cited claim is checked and labeled. Everything runs in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

Without a key, existing sources are honestly labeled cited (support not checked). With a key, each abstract is compared against its claim — partial or unclear cases still downgrade, never pretend.

§2

Verify a citation

A single identifier, checked against the registries directly. No server, no signup.

Queries CrossRef / DataCite / arXiv live. A result of cited means the source exists — it does not mean the source supports your claim.

§3

The honesty contract

Five labels, three tags. No sixth option, no hedging.

ABSTRACT-SUPPORTED
The source exists and its abstract substantively supports the claim's key assertion. Full text is not checked — supporting evidence may live in the paper body.
Example: "LLMs fabricate 23.4% of citations in academic drafts" — matched against the cited paper's abstract.
CITED
The source exists, but support is unchecked or only partial. The author decides.
Example: "The dataset contains 1.2M annotated examples (Smith et al.)" — real source, content not yet compared.
UNVERIFIED
No record found, the marker cannot be resolved, or the match is ambiguous. Do not cite it as-is.
Example: "Our method outperforms all baselines on every benchmark" — an author assertion with no source attached.
FABRICATED
An explicit identifier (DOI / arXiv ID) checked against its registry and found not to exist — fabricated or mistyped. Absence of evidence is UNVERIFIED; this is evidence of absence.
RATE_LIMITED
Sources were rate-limited while checking — not checked, not proven fake. A limit is never presented as a fabrication.
[STALE:YYYY] [CHIMERIC:author-mismatch] [RETRACTED]
Tags, not labels: stale data, a real title carrying the wrong authors, a retracted paper. A retracted paper can never be ABSTRACT-SUPPORTED.
[SPECIFIC-NOT-IN-ABSTRACT] [POSSIBLE-MISATTRIBUTION]
Anti-paraphrase tags: the claim leans on hard specifics (numbers, quoted phrases) absent from the abstract. For the second, the missing specific was found in a paper this work cites — the finding may originate there. Both cap the claim at CITED.

The governing rule is downgrade-only: uncertainty always resolves toward the weaker label. A tool that cannot verify must say so.

§4

From idea to acceptance

veripaper is a workbench, not a writing tool. Each phase hands a typed contract to the next.

  1. Proposal decision. Compare candidate directions: heat index from top venues, citation forecast, frontier-extrapolation for data-scarce bets. §0 above is live in v0.1.
  2. Idea distillation. From rough notes to a concept note: novelty check, contribution statement, research questions.
  3. Evidence grounding. Every claim gets an honest label — §1 and §2 above. Live in v0.1.
  4. Top-venue benchmarking. Measured against real accepted papers from top venues — not imagined standards.
  5. Reviewer simulation. Pre-submission critique in the voice of the target venue. Evidence gaps first.
  6. Venue targeting. Acceptance-probability estimates and submission strategy. A wrong venue costs months.
  7. Rebuttal & acceptance loop. Point-by-point responses and revision tracking, so acceptance compounds.
§5

Principles

  1. Lookup, not generation. The tool queries registries; it never writes your argument for you.
  2. Downgrade, never pretend. Uncertain evidence gets the weaker label. The author always has the last word.
  3. Your keys, your data. Optional BYOK for content judgment. Drafts never leave the browser.
  4. Open and auditable. MIT licensed. Every lookup, label rule, and threshold is in the source.
§6

§7

Where to submit

Describe the topic. Get a ranked venue shortlist with fit reasons — a starting filter, not a prediction.

§8

Rebuttal manager

Paste the reviews. Each point is split out, classified, and given a response template. Track what is addressed — export when done.

State is saved in this browser (localStorage). The tool structures the work; the science in each response is yours.
